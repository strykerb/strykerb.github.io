01 — Featured project

NeoPoi Lightshow Editor

A desktop app for building music-synchronized lightshows for LED poi. I designed and built it with an AI-assisted workflow, and I use it to make the shows I perform.

  • Creative tools
  • Audio analysis
  • Generative AI
  • Pixel art
  • Hardware

The problem

LED poi don't have a screen. Each one has a single strip of LEDs that shows a narrow slice of an image at a time. Spin it, and your eye stitches those slices into a full picture. That makes the art awkward to author: images are tiny, they have to loop cleanly around a circle, and they need to change in time with the music.

Doing that by hand for a whole song takes a long time. I wanted a tool that would do the tedious parts, like finding the beats and sections and planning the changes, and leave me the creative decisions.

1

Song map

Local analysis on my machine finds tempo and beat candidates, silences, change points, and stutters. Optionally, the audio goes to Gemini, which labels sections (intro, verse, chorus…) with energy levels and short descriptions. Every section can be edited, split, merged, or dragged on the timeline.

2

Show direction

I choose how busy the show should be, for example a new image on every beat with strobes on stutters. The editor then turns the song map into a plan of image changes across both poi.

3

Images

Looping pixel-art images sit in a lane for each poi. The live stage previews them as spinning discs, in sync with the audio, so I can judge a section before it ever reaches the hardware.

4

Device & export

The finished sequence is exported to the physical poi, where it plays in time with the music.

How an image becomes a lightshow

The poi only ever lights one column. Each column is drawn at the angle the poi has reached, so the image wraps into a disc: columns become angles, rows become distance from your hand. Slow it down to see the slices.

Why I care about this one

It's the most complete thing I've made on my own. An unusual idea went through tool and UI design, AI-assisted programming, audio analysis, and a hardware constraint, and came out as shows I actually perform. I made every product decision, and the tool kept changing as I used it.

05 — Flow arts

Poi_Fish

Outside of work I spin poi: LED poi, fire poi, and silk. I choreograph sets to music and post them as Poi_Fish.

It's also where a lot of my design taste comes from. A good spin and a good game interaction have similar requirements: readable motion, timing that lands on the beat, and a short loop between trying something and seeing it. NeoPoi exists because I wanted better tools for my own art.

06 — About

About me

My favorite part of the work is first contact with a new capability: an eye tracker, an EMG band, a haptic actuator, a model that can describe a song. Figuring out what it's actually good for usually means building something quickly, putting it in someone's hands, and changing it until it feels right.

I studied computer science with a focus on game design at UC Santa Cruz. Since then I've worked as a research prototyper and as a production game engineer, and I use AI tools heavily in how I build. Lately I've been most interested in real-time generative models and the kinds of play they could make possible.

Away from screens: circus arts (aerial silks, trapeze, lyra), jiu-jitsu, tennis, and taking my cat Salem to places cats don't usually go.
